Dwayne Johnson

Roots

Born into wrestling

Dwayne Douglas Johnson was born on 2 May 1972 in Hayward, California, into wrestling royalty on both sides. His father, “Soul Man” Rocky Johnson, was a Black Canadian wrestler who in 1983 won the WWF Tag Team Championship with Tony Atlas, making them the first Black tag team champions in the company’s history. His mother, Ata Johnson, is the daughter of the Samoan wrestler High Chief Peter Maivia, and his grandmother Lia Maivia later ran a wrestling promotion in Hawaii.

He grew up in locker rooms and on road trips, watching his father work. Years later he would carry both family names into the ring, first as “Rocky Maivia”, then simply as The Rock.

Early life

A kid who kept moving

His father’s career kept the family on the move. As a young boy he lived briefly with his mother’s family in Grey Lynn, Auckland, in New Zealand, then in Hamden, Connecticut, where he went to elementary school, and later in Nashville, Tennessee, and Honolulu, Hawaii. Money was always tight.

Johnson has described the moment it bottomed out: at 14, in Honolulu, the family came home to find themselves locked out of the $120-a-week efficiency apartment they could no longer afford, with an eviction notice on the door. He has also spoken openly about his mother attempting to take her own life when he was 15, a memory he says shaped how he thinks about mental health.

He was angry and often in trouble. By 17 he had been arrested several times for fighting, theft and check fraud. He has said that the eviction became a turning point, and that the weight room became the place he could control.

The family settled in Bethlehem, Pennsylvania. At Freedom High School, football coach Jody Cwik saw athletic potential in him. Johnson tells the story of first meeting Cwik when the coach caught him using the teachers’ restroom, and of Cwik turning that confrontation into a recruitment pitch. He became a standout defensive tackle, drew Division I scholarship offers, and chose the University of Miami.

Football

Miami, Calgary and seven bucks

At the University of Miami (1990–1994) he played defensive tackle, wearing No. 94, and was part of the Hurricanes squad that won the 1991 national championship. He spent most of his career in backup roles, stuck behind elite players on the depth chart, among them the future Hall of Famer Warren Sapp, and he has talked about the injuries that set him back.

In 1995 he graduated with a Bachelor of General Studies, a dual major in criminology and physiology. No NFL team drafted him. He signed with the Calgary Stampeders of the Canadian Football League and was put on the practice roster, a job he has said paid about $250 a week, and he was cut two months into the 1995 season.

He flew home to his parents in Tampa with, as he tells it, seven dollars to his name, and he has called the months that followed the lowest point of his life and a period of real depression. It was then that he asked his father to train him for the ring. The number stayed with him: it later gave his production company its name, Seven Bucks.

Wrestling

The ring

He asked his father to train him. Rocky Johnson was reluctant, warning him how hard the business was, then agreed and trained him himself. With help from the WWF veteran Pat Patterson he got a tryout, and within a year he was on the biggest stage in the business.

  1. 1996

    Flex Kavana, then Rocky Maivia

    He wrestled in Memphis as “Flex Kavana” and won the USWA tag team titles. In November he debuted at WWF Survivor Series as Rocky Maivia, a smiling, clean-cut hero named after his father and grandfather, and was the last man standing in his first match.

  2. 1997

    Setback

    Champion, and booed

    He won the Intercontinental Championship three months in, yet crowds turned on the character, chanting “Rocky sucks” and “Die, Rocky, die”. After a knee injury he came back angry, joined the Nation of Domination and began calling himself The Rock. The boos turned to cheers.

  3. 1998

    The People’s Champion

    He took over the Nation, then won his first WWF Championship at Survivor Series, beating Mankind in the final of a one-night tournament. The raised eyebrow, “If you smell what The Rock is cooking” and the People’s Elbow made him a phenomenon.

  4. 1999–2001

    The Attitude Era

    He headlined WrestleMania XV and WrestleMania X-Seven against Stone Cold Steve Austin, the rivalry that defined the era. He won the 2000 Royal Rumble and traded the title with Triple H, Kurt Angle and Chris Jericho.

  5. 2002

    Icon vs. Icon

    At WrestleMania X8 in Toronto he beat Hulk Hogan in front of a crowd that cheered the legend, one of the most famous matches ever staged. The same year, The Scorpion King made him a leading man.

  6. 2003

    Hollywood Rock

    Playing a cocky “Hollywood” villain, he finally beat Stone Cold at WrestleMania XIX, which turned out to be Austin’s last match. A few weeks later he lost to Goldberg at Backlash.

  7. 2004

    Setback

    Leaving the ring

    After teaming with Mick Foley in a losing effort against Evolution at WrestleMania XX, he stepped away to act full time. Many fans felt he had turned his back on wrestling, and the criticism followed him for years.

  8. 2011–2013

    Once in a lifetime

    He came back to host WrestleMania XXVII, then beat John Cena in the main event of WrestleMania XXVIII, billed as “Once in a Lifetime”. In 2013 he beat CM Punk for the WWE Championship, eleven years after his previous world title, before losing it to Cena at WrestleMania 29.

  9. 2024

    The Final Boss

    He joined the board of WWE’s parent company, TKO, in January 2024. When the storyline set him up to face his cousin Roman Reigns at WrestleMania XL instead of Cody Rhodes, fans revolted. He leaned into the boos, turned villain as “The Final Boss” and teamed with Reigns to win the event’s opening night.

REINVENTION & DEFINING PIVOTS

Crossroads that built the legacy

Every era of Dwayne Johnson’s career was forged at a real-world crossroads—moments where a setback, creative risk, or structural shift demanded complete reinvention.

1995

The $7 Bucks Release (Calgary to Tampa)

Cut from the Calgary Stampeders practice squad two days into the 1995 CFL season, he flew home to Tampa and emptied his wallet to find a five, a one, and change—seven dollars. Rather than walking away from athletics, he asked his father Rocky Johnson to train him for the ring, turning that rock-bottom number into the lifelong touchstone and name of Seven Bucks Productions.

1997

From Rocky Maivia to The Rock

When arenas rejected his clean-cut rookie persona following his Survivor Series debut, he asked following a knee injury for two minutes on the microphone to speak with unfiltered honesty. Joining the Nation of Domination and referring to himself in the third person, he transformed crowd resistance into the most electrifying character in sports entertainment history.

2004

The Full-Time Leap to Cinema

At age 32, at the height of his Attitude Era fame, he let his WWE contract expire to pursue a full-time acting career—determined to build a long-term film catalog without a safety net, before returning home seven years later to headline WrestleMania XXVIII in Miami (“Once in a Lifetime”).

2010–2012

Reclaiming His Identity & Co-Founding Seven Bucks

After several years of studio formulas that asked him to slim down and distance himself from his wrestling roots, he restructured his team, partnered with Dany Garcia as his manager, returned to intense conditioning, stepped into Fast Five (2011) as Luke Hobbs, and co-founded Seven Bucks Productions in 2012 to take ownership of his slate.

2022–2024

Franchise Resets, TKO Board Seat & “The Final Boss”

Navigating studio leadership transitions in Hollywood while expanding his ownership footprint, he joined the Board of Directors of TKO Group Holdings in January 2024, secured full ownership of “The Rock” trademark and intellectual property, and listened directly to the WWE audience ahead of WrestleMania XL—pivoting into the ruthless “Final Boss” persona to help deliver the biggest WrestleMania in company history.

2025–2026

The A24 Dramatic Evolution (The Smashing Machine)

Choosing artistic vulnerability over guaranteed blockbuster comfort, he collaborated with writer-director Benny Safdie and A24 to portray UFC pioneer Mark Kerr in The Smashing Machine. The performance earned a 15-minute standing ovation at the Venice Film Festival and his first Golden Globe nomination for Best Actor in a Motion Picture – Drama.

Hollywood & Enterprise Empire

A second career, then a third

1999–2005

Early breakout

He made his acting debut playing his own father in an episode of That ’70s Show (1999), then appeared as the Scorpion King in The Mummy Returns (2001). Its spin-off, The Scorpion King (2002), paid him $5.5 million, a Guinness record for an actor’s first leading role. Arnold Schwarzenegger’s cameo in The Rundown (2003) played as a passing of the action-hero torch, followed by Walking Tall (2004), Be Cool and Doom (both 2005).

2006–2010

Reinvention and the family pivot

Gridiron Gang and Southland Tales (2006) gave way to family comedies such as The Game Plan (2007), Race to Witch Mountain (2009) and Tooth Fairy (2010), plus Get Smart (2008), Faster and The Other Guys (2010). Deciding to stop downplaying his size and wrestling heritage, he aligned with Dany Garcia to build a global production and franchise model around his authentic identity.

2011–2021

Box-office titan

As Luke Hobbs he re-energised the Fast & Furious franchise in Fast Five (2011), Fast & Furious 6 (2013), Furious 7 (2015, $1.515 billion worldwide), The Fate of the Furious (2017) and Hobbs & Shaw (2019). Around it came San Andreas, Central Intelligence, the voice of Maui in Disney’s Moana (2016), HBO’s Ballers (2015–2019), two Jumanji hits, Rampage, Skyscraper, Jungle Cruise and Netflix’s Red Notice (2021).

2022–2026

The A24 dramatic turn & global leadership

Following Black Adam (2022), Red One (2024), and the record-breaking animated sequel Moana 2 (2024), he transformed into UFC pioneer Mark Kerr for Benny Safdie’s A24 drama The Smashing Machine (2025), earning a Golden Globe nomination for Best Actor – Drama, before bringing Maui to life in person in Disney’s live-action Moana (2026).

Filmography

    Off camera he co-founded Seven Bucks Productions with his long-time business partner Dany Garcia, founded Teremana Small Batch Tequila, PAPATUI Men’s Care, ZOA Energy, and the Project Rock performance division with Under Armour. As Co-Owner of the United Football League (UFL) and a member of the Board of Directors of TKO Group Holdings, he bridges entertainment, sports ownership, and global consumer brands.

    Values

    What he stands for

    1. Family first

      He is a father of three daughters: Simone, who now wrestles in WWE as Ava, and Jasmine and Tiana with his wife, Lauren Hashian. He and his first wife, Dany Garcia, have kept a close friendship and business partnership since their divorce, which he often points to as a model for co-parenting.

    2. Outwork everyone

      His most repeated motto is to be the hardest worker in the room. He trains before dawn and credits discipline, not talent, for what he has built.

    3. Gratitude and humility

      Naming his company Seven Bucks keeps the lowest moment of his life in view. He regularly thanks fans by name and stays close to the Polynesian and Black communities he comes from.

    4. Talking about mental health

      He has been unusually open about depression, after being cut from football and after his mother’s suicide attempt, and has encouraged people, especially men, to talk about it and ask for help.

    5. Giving back

      He is one of Make-A-Wish’s most active celebrity supporters and has visited troops for more than two decades, from Edwards Air Force Base in 2002 to the Pentagon in 2023.
